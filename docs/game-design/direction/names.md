# The name

The brief said to forget *sphaze*, and it should be forgotten: it names
the prototype's one gimmick (a maze on a sphere), and in this direction
the sphere is the *starting cage* — the thing the game is about leaving.
A title naming the first hour is a title that ages badly by hour three.

What the name has to carry: **something that exists without a cause.**
Ideally it also carries the melancholy, since all three endings are
bittersweet.

A convenient fact: **the mathematical field's own vocabulary is already
theological.** "Garden of Eden", "orphan", "quiescent", "ash" are the
literature's actual technical terms, not decoration added by us. So a
title in that register is faithful to the source material rather than
reaching for gravitas.

## Recommendation: **UNBEGOTTEN**

*Unbegotten* means: having no origin, not generated, not produced by
anything. It is the precise English word for the property the entire game
is about, and it happens to be the theological term for the same idea
(Nicene usage, of the Father) — which sits exactly alongside "Garden of
Eden" as the field's own borrowed vocabulary.

- **Exact.** It states the win condition and the theme in one word.
- **Ownable.** Essentially zero competition in games or film; instant
  search ownership, which is worth real money to an indie release.
- **Right register.** Cold, old, a little sad. Matches a contemplative
  game about determinism and freedom, and matches the three endings.
- **It sounds like a title**, not like a word.

Risks, honestly: it reads religious to some, which may misdirect; it is
slightly archaic; a minority will hesitate over pronunciation
(un-be-GOT-en).

## The alternates

### ORPHAN
The literal technical term: a finite pattern with no predecessor is called
an *orphan*. Short, human, emotionally direct, and it works on both levels
at once.
**Against:** a badly crowded namespace (a well-known horror film, several
games), so search and store discoverability are a permanent fight. This is
the safe, accessible pick and it is a coin flip against the
recommendation — decide it with a trademark and store search, not taste
alone.

### AMENABLE
The actual property that has to fail for you to be free — and in ordinary
English it means *compliant, docile, willing to yield*. The mathematics
and the everyday word mean the same thing here, which is the best pun
available in the entire subject.
**It has the best tagline of any option:** *"A world where everything has
a cause. Be the exception."*
**Against:** as a title it sounds soft and slightly corporate; it carries
no intrigue for anyone who does not already know the term. Best-in-class
idea, worst-in-class shelf appeal.

### NO PREDECESSOR
Stark and exactly the definition. Reads well on a capsule.
**Against:** faintly clinical, and it points at sci-fi thriller rather
than contemplative.

### SEVENFOLD
From the `{7,3}` heptagrid — seven-sided cells. Evocative, mysterious,
free of jargon.
**Against:** carries the geometry half and none of the causation half, and
a well-known band owns the search term.

### Also considered, and why not
- **STILL LIFE** — lovely double meaning, but it names the *failure* state
  (the ones who stopped), and the namespace is very crowded.
- **THE QUIESCENT** — beautiful, but it names the dead background rather
  than the player.
- **ASH** — thematically apt (what a settled world becomes) and far too
  crowded.
- **THE FIFTH POSTULATE** — carries the geometry precisely, carries the
  automaton not at all.

## Before committing

1. Trademark search and a Steam/itch search for collisions.
2. Check the domain and the social handle.
3. Say it out loud to someone who knows nothing about the game and see
   what they think it is about. If the answer is interesting *and wrong*,
   that is usually fine. If it is boring, drop it.
4. The repo rename is cheap now and expensive later — the module is
   `sphaze` in the stack's `games` module, the GHCR package is
   `ghcr.io/platypod/sphaze`, and both are referenced from the deploy
   docs. Do it in one pass when the name lands.
