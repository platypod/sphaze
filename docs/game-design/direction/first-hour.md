# The first hour

A design is a hypothesis; the opening hour is where it is falsified. This
is a beat-by-beat of the intended experience — **no tutorial, no text, no
UI, nothing announced** — written to prove the systems in
[systems.md](systems.md) can teach themselves.

Two rules held throughout: *every lesson is learned by making a mistake or
by paying attention*, and *nothing that happens is explained*.

---

### 0-4 min · Wake — "there is a here"

You are standing. The light is warm — amber, brass, ember. (You will not
know for six hours that warmth means κ > 0. You will know it *felt*
different when you first see cold light.)

You can walk. That is all you can do. Walking is unremarkable and the
ground is unremarkable, which is deliberate: the game needs one minute of
ordinariness to make the next thing land.

**Then you raise your head.**

The ground does not stop at a horizon. It curves *up*, and over, and
closes above you — and across the interior, impossibly far and perfectly
legible, is more ground. Ground with things on it. You are inside
something.

*Taught: the space is closed, and looking up is the primary verb.*
*Untaught: everything else.*

### 4-12 min · The pulse — "there is a when"

Walking, you notice the world has a beat. Not music: a *pulse*. Cells
underfoot brighten and darken on it. Distant lights on the far side
flicker in the same rhythm — which means the whole world shares one clock,
and you can verify that yourself by watching two places at once. Only a
closed space lets you do that. The geometry is already doing work.

You come to a passage that is closed. You wait — because there is nothing
else to do — and on some beat, it opens.

*Taught: the world ticks, the tick is global, and waiting is a legitimate
action.*

This is also the first appearance of the game's core courtesy: **it is
never in a hurry, and neither are you.**

### 12-22 min · The socket — "I can write"

You find a pentagon. It is the first ornamented thing you have seen —
everything else has been regular, and this is *detailed*, which reads as
important without a marker floating over it.

Standing on it, the game offers its one and only prompt in the entire
runtime: a key. Press it and the camera settles toward the floor, where an
engraving of the local cells sits waiting. Click cells. Leave.

Whatever you drew, the world takes. On the next tick it changes. On the
one after that it changes again — and *keeps* changing, several cells
beyond where you drew, in a direction you did not choose.

*Taught: you can write into the world, and what you write has consequences
you do not control.*

This is the thesis of the entire game delivered as a mechanic in ten
seconds, and no one has said the word "cause".

### 22-32 min · The thing you made — "it left"

Whatever you drew probably died. Draw again. Eventually something *walks*
— a small configuration that moves away across the surface, stepping, one
cell at a time, on the beat.

You follow it. It outpaces you, or it doesn't, and either way you watch it
for a while because it is the most interesting thing that has happened.

You watch it complete one full period.

**Nothing is announced.** But you have just learned it, in the strict
sense of [systems.md](systems.md)'s `READ` verb — and the game will not
tell you that for another ten minutes, when you need it.

*Taught: patterns travel. Watching is not idle.*

### 32-42 min · The ghost — "someone is here"

Off the path, in a hollow, something is looping. Not travelling — a
configuration returning to itself, over and over, on a period of a few
seconds.

Approach it and it *responds*. Some greeting. A few words, warm, slightly
formal, and it asks you something.

Walk away and come back.

**It greets you identically.** Same words, same warmth, same question.

*Taught: nothing, mechanically. Everything, otherwise.* This is the hook
for Thread 2 and it is planted at minute forty because it needs the rest
of the game to detonate. The player will not know for hours that they have
met an oscillator, that an oscillator is a pattern that survived by
looping, and that looping costs you the ability to learn. Right now it is
just a slightly upsetting thing in a hole.

### 42-52 min · The return — "I have been here before"

> **Replaced 2026-08-12.** This beat used to be "The gap — *I can be
> something else*", where a chasm forced the player into a glider body and
> taught that bodies are trades. `BECOME` was cut after Phase 0, so the
> lesson had to change. What went in its place teaches the game's *actual*
> progression grammar, which the old version never did.

The path loops back past somewhere you have already been — the socket, or
the hollow with the looping thing in it. Nothing here has changed.

**You have.**

The pattern you watched complete its cycle at minute 30 is running here
too, and now you can see that it is the *same* pattern. Not similar:
identical, on a different clock. You could not have seen that an hour ago
because you had not yet watched one long enough to know its shape.

Nothing was unlocked. Nothing was awarded. A place you already walked
through has simply become **legible**, because you understand more than
you did.

*Taught, and this is the single most important lesson in the game: the
world does not open up, you do. Every gate in the next fourteen hours
works exactly like this one.*

### 52-60 min · The reveal — "what am I, then?"

> **Also revised 2026-08-12.** The reveal used to be "I am one too" —
> the camera pulls back and you are five cells, the same kind of object as
> the glider you drew. That was true under `BECOME`, where the player
> climbed the taxonomy from the bottom. Now the player is *far past* the
> primitives, so the same camera move has to deliver a much better beat.

Somewhere high, or at the antipode, or wherever the level design has been
patiently steering you: a vantage.

The camera pulls back. Past the walking scale. Past the point where you
are a figure.

The world resolves into cells — and so do **you**. But where the thing you
drew at the socket was five cells, you are... not. You are enormous.
Intricate. Structured in ways nothing else in view comes close to, and
plainly made of exactly the same substance as the blinking simple things
you have spent an hour learning to read.

The hour does not end on an answer. It ends on the question that runs the
rest of the game:

> **How does something like me come out of rules like these?**

Earned rather than announced — and every primitive form the player has
just learned to recognise is now, retroactively, a piece of evidence.

---

## What the hour has established

| | |
|---|---|
| **Mechanically** | walk, look, tick, seed, read, zoom — 6 of 7 verbs, none explained |
| **Geometrically** | closed space; one global clock; things travel; the far side is legible and the near side is not |
| **Narratively** | you can write; what you write escapes you; someone was here before and something is wrong with them; you are made of the same stuff as everything else — and vastly more of it |
| **Emotionally** | curiosity, one small dread, one unanswerable question |

**Nothing has been locked, and nothing has been unlocked** — literally
nothing, in this version. Every place the player reached was reachable
from minute one, and the only thing that changed in an hour is what they
can *see* when they get there. That is Pillar 3 in its strongest form,
and it is why minute 42 is a *return* rather than a gate.

## Where the real game opens

The hour closes the tutorial the player never noticed and opens the actual
question, which is not *what am I* but **how did something like me get
here**. The sphere is warm, closed, kind, and finite; the player has just
found out they are a pattern in it, and a far more complicated one than
anything around them; and the next thing they should find — within
another hour — is the first evidence that the warmth is deliberate.

That is Thread 3, and the answer is that the prison is a cradle.
